The Real Problem

Vendor days should not force you to guess.

VendorFlowDS is being shaped around the questions small sellers ask before a big event, especially when handmade items need to stay separate from regular retail goods.

How do I avoid overmaking inventory?

Take preorders first, buy blanks from real demand, and make only confirmed shirts, totes, and hats plus a few samples.

How do I cover upfront event costs?

Preorder proceeds can help cover vendor applications, booth deposits, blanks, transfers, packaging, and the supplies needed before the event opens.

How do I keep event stock separate?

VendorFlow only tracks the items I intend to bring to the market or event, so handmade inventory stays separate from regular retail goods.

Can I sell while producing live?

Yes. The booth can become the production moment: heat press shirts and bags, patch-press hats, and let customers see the work happen.

How does a helper know what to do?

The queue shows what is new, what needs pressing, what is ready, what goes on the pickup rack, and what still needs shipping.

How do customers avoid waiting around?

Each order can get a pickup number or status link so customers can browse, leave, or come back when their order is ready.

How does this prove VendorFlowDS?

The souvenir shop becomes the case study: real products, real preorders, real pickup flow, and real event-day lessons.
